Welcome to my MSc Data Science e-portfolio! 

This portfolio records my progress through the MSc, module by module. It brings together the activities, discussions, projects and reflections that show both the technical skills I am gaining and how I connect them to my background in information science.

**Professional Background**

I am an information professional with twelve years of experience across public, higher education, business and health libraries, in Uruguay and the Netherlands. Since 2020 I have worked as a Librarian at a Higher Education institute, where I support students and staff with all library-related services, such as: books and journals requests, literature and bibliometric searches, referencing and copyright, information literacy and the institution’s open repository of MSc and PhD theses.

In the past I have also worked as a research librarian for an international consultancy company, managing a corporate research database, and as a librarian for a medical professional association, where I built a new library catalogue for the organisation’s library together with a software engineer. Across all of my job experiences, my work has centred on organising information so that people can find it and use it.

My research focuses on information behaviour, particularly that of minorities and small, often invisible communities. I am interested in how people and information relate to each other: how people’s information worlds take shape, and how their contexts affect the way they seek and use information.

**Qualifications**

* BA in Library Sciences, Universidad de la República (Uruguay)  
* MA in Library and Information Services Management, University of Sheffield Information School, awarded with Distinction. Winner of the iSchool’s Learning Light Prize 2018–19 for the best dissertation in the field of e-learning.


**Why Data Science**

Libraries have always been about data: describing it, structuring it and making it findable. Studying Data Science lets me take that foundation further, building practical skills in Python, data wrangling, database design and visualisation, and understanding how large datasets can be cleaned, managed and turned into evidence for decision-making. My goal is to become a “full-stack” researcher, equally at home with qualitative and quantitative methods, and to move my career towards behavioural research, both in information use and in other fields such as consumer and user behaviour.
